# Team and Access Control

The **Members** page in the Organization group of the Stacktape Console controls who can use your organization and
what they reach. Invite people, choose their role, projects and AWS accounts, require two-factor sign-in, give CI an
organization key that does not belong to a person, and see what stops working before you remove someone.

## Overview

The page has up to four parts:

- **Two-factor sign-in** (Owners and Admins): whether members who sign in with a password must also use an
  authenticator app.
- **People**: everyone who has joined, with their role, what a Developer or Viewer reaches, two-factor status, when
  they were last active and when they joined. Search by name or email, filter by role, or show only members without
  two-factor or inactive for 30 days or more.
- **Invitations**: invitations that have not been accepted yet, who sent them, and when they expire.
- **Organization keys** (Owners and Admins): API keys that belong to the organization, for CI.

Click a member's name to open their page. Owners and Admins can open anyone's page; Developers and Viewers can open
their own.

## Roles

| Role | Can do | Reaches |
|------|--------|---------|
| Owner | Everything, including deleting the organization, billing and appointing other Owners | Every project and every connected AWS account |
| Admin | Everything except deleting the organization | Every project and every connected AWS account |
| Developer | Deploy, delete non-production stages, manage secrets and parameters | Only the projects and AWS accounts chosen for them |
| Viewer | Read projects, deployments, logs, incidents and members | Only the projects and AWS accounts chosen for them |

## Project and AWS account access

A Developer or Viewer has two lists, set when you invite them and changed with **Edit access**:

- **Projects** decide which projects they see in the Console and which projects the CLI lets them deploy.
- **AWS accounts** decide where Stacktape gives them AWS access: CLI credentials for deploys, scripts and stack
  commands, deployments started from the Console, and the Console pages that read AWS directly (logs, metrics,
  secrets, parameters, debug sessions, redeploys and rollbacks, Investigate and Fix with AI).

<Warning>

AWS access always covers the whole account. A Developer given the account `production` for the project `shop` can,
with the credentials the CLI receives, also reach other projects' stacks in `production`. Give a person an account
only if they may act in all of it. Agencies with one AWS account per client get a clean boundary by giving each
person only their clients' accounts.

</Warning>

A Developer with no AWS accounts can still see their projects in the Console, but the CLI refuses to deploy, and the
pages that read AWS say that their access does not include the account. Owners and Admins always reach every account.

## Inviting members

1. Open **Members** and click **Invite members**.
2. Enter one or more email addresses, separated by commas or new lines (up to 20 at once).
3. Choose a role. For a Developer or Viewer, choose their projects and AWS accounts.
4. Click **Send invitations**.

Someone who already has a Stacktape account becomes a member at once. Anyone else gets an email and appears under
**Invitations** until they sign up. An address that is already a member, or that could not be invited, stays in the
field with the reason, so the rest are not lost.

An invitation works for 14 days after its email last went out. **Resend invitation** sends it again and starts the
14 days again; an expired invitation can be resent the same way. **Copy invite link** copies the sign-up link to
share another way; it only works for the invited address. **Cancel invitation** stops the link from working.

Personal organizations have exactly one member. To work with others, create a team organization; team organizations
have no member limit.

## Organization keys for CI

A personal [API key](/stacktape-console/api-keys) acts as the person who created it and stops working when they leave
the organization. CI that uses one breaks when that person is removed. An organization key belongs to the
organization instead:

1. In **Organization keys**, click **New organization key**.
2. Name it after where it runs, for example `github-actions`.
3. Choose **Developer** with the projects and AWS accounts the pipeline deploys, or **Admin** for everything.
4. Copy the key from the next dialog; it is shown only once. Store it as the `STACKTAPE_API_KEY` secret of your CI.

The list shows who created each key and when it was last used. Revoking a key stops every pipeline that uses it at
once. Organization keys cannot create organizations or connect AI subscriptions, which belong to people.

## Requiring two-factor sign-in

Owners and Admins can require two-factor sign-in with **Require** in the Two-factor sign-in row. The confirmation lists
the members who sign in with a password only. After that, those members see only a screen that sends them to
[Account Settings](/stacktape-console/account-settings) to add an authenticator app; the rest of the organization opens
again as soon as they do. Signing in with Google counts as two-factor. API keys, including the CLI and CI, keep
working.

You need two-factor sign-in on your own account before you can require it.

## Removing a member

Open the member's row menu, or their page, and choose **Remove**. The dialog lists what stops working with them:

- their personal API keys, with when each was last used,
- their own AI subscriptions for incident runs,
- their Slack link.

If one of their keys was used in the last 7 days, the dialog says so, because CI may depend on it, and offers to
create an organization key first. Stacks they deployed keep running, their deployments stay in the history, and
organization keys keep working, including ones they created.

**Revoke API keys** in the row menu revokes a member's personal keys without removing them.

## Last active

The Last active column shows the most recent of three times, listed separately when you hover it and on the member's
page:

- **Console**: their last visit, recorded at most once an hour.
- **CLI**: the last request with one of their personal API keys.
- **Last deploy**: the last deploy they started.

## Troubleshooting

### "Your access does not include the AWS account"

The member is a Developer or Viewer and the account is not in their AWS accounts. An Owner or Admin adds it with
**Edit access** on the Members page. The CLI shows the same reason, and `stacktape` lists only the accounts the member
can use.

### The CLI stopped deploying for a Developer after an upgrade

Developers and Viewers reach only the AWS accounts chosen for them. When this was introduced, each of them was given
the accounts their projects had deployed to; an account their projects never used must be added with **Edit access**.

### Invite members is disabled

Either your role cannot invite (only Owners and Admins can), or this is a personal organization.

### An invitation shows Expired

Its 14 days passed. Choose **Resend invitation**; the person can sign up for 14 more days.
