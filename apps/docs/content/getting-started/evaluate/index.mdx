This page is for the person who decides whether Stacktape fits the team for the next few years. Each section gives the
short answer and links to the page with the details.

## What it is and what it isn't

Stacktape turns one configuration file into a CloudFormation stack in your own AWS account. It has two parts:

- **The CLI** is open source under the MIT license. It writes the config with you (`stacktape init`), packages your
  code, deploys and deletes stacks, runs dev mode and reads logs, metrics and secrets. It works with your AWS
  credentials and needs no Stacktape account.
- **The Console** is an optional hosted service for teams: deploy history, costs, error tracking, incidents, uptime
  checks, Git deployments, team access and an audit log.

Stacktape is not a hosting provider. Your app runs on AWS services in your account: Lambda, ECS on Fargate, RDS,
DynamoDB, S3, CloudFront and others; see the [resource catalog](/resources). It supports only AWS. It has no
Kubernetes and no runtime of its own between your code and AWS. When the catalog doesn't cover something, you can add
[raw CloudFormation, CDK constructs or overrides](/configuration/overrides-and-escape-hatches) to the same stack.

## What runs where

| Part | Where it runs | Who pays |
|---|---|---|
| Your app: functions, containers, databases, buckets, CDN | Your AWS account, one CloudFormation stack per stage | You pay AWS |
| MongoDB Atlas and Upstash resources, if you use them | The provider's account | You pay the provider |
| The CLI, builds and deploys | Your machine or your CI | Free |
| Git deployment build runners | An EC2 instance in your AWS account | You pay AWS |
| Uptime check probes and the error detector | Small Lambda functions in your AWS account | You pay AWS |
| The Console and its database | Stacktape's AWS account, in `eu-west-1` (Ireland) | Stacktape |
| The Console connection | An IAM role in your account that Stacktape assumes | Free |

Your stacks keep running if you stop using the CLI or the Console. See [Stop using Stacktape](/getting-started/evaluate/stop-using-stacktape).

## Who does what in production

Take an API container with a Postgres database, as in the [example on the home page](/).

- **AWS** runs the servers under Fargate and RDS, applies database maintenance in the maintenance window and replaces
  failed hardware.
- **Stacktape** turns your config into the stack: network, security groups, IAM roles, the load balancer or API
  gateway, logs and the alarms you configure. It builds your code and deploys it. With the Console, it also groups
  errors into incidents and runs uptime checks.
- **Your team** decides the sizes and scaling limits, sets backup retention and tests a restore, approves production
  deploys in its own pipeline and answers alerts. Your team also owns the AWS account: who can sign in to it and what
  it may spend.

[Going to production](/getting-started/going-to-production) lists what to set up before real users arrive.

## What leaves your AWS account

Without the Console, Stacktape receives two things: anonymous CLI usage telemetry, which one environment variable
turns off, and the generated config that `stacktape init` sends to the price estimator. A coding agent you pick in init
sends the files it reads to its own provider.

With the Console, some features send operational data to Stacktape: errors with their masked log line, alarm state
changes, uptime results, monthly cost reports and security findings. Application logs, build artifacts and secrets
stay in your account. The Console stores what it receives in `eu-west-1`. Error samples are kept with no time limit.
[What leaves your AWS account](/observability/overview#what-leaves-your-aws-account) lists every feature, field and
retention period.

## Access and security

- **AWS access.** Without the Console, Stacktape has no access to your account. Connecting the Console creates an IAM
  role that only Stacktape's AWS account can assume, with an external ID and sessions of at most 12 hours. The role has
  `AdministratorAccess`, so the Console can deploy anything a stack contains. Each workload runs with its own role
  that allows only what its config declares. See [AWS permissions](/stacktape-console/aws-permissions).
- **People.** Members are Owners, Admins, Developers or Viewers. Developers and Viewers see only the projects and AWS
  accounts you give them. See [Team and access control](/stacktape-console/team-and-access-control).
- **Sign-in.** Owners and Admins can require two-factor sign-in on every plan. Single sign-on (SAML or OIDC) and SCIM come with
  the Flexible plan. See [Single sign-on](/stacktape-console/single-sign-on).
- **Audit.** The audit log keeps Stacktape events for 400 days and shows AWS CloudTrail events from the last 90 days.
  Every AWS call the Console makes for a person carries their identity. See [Audit log](/stacktape-console/audit-log).

## Accounts, stages and isolation

A stage, such as `dev` or `production`, is a separate CloudFormation stack with its own functions, containers,
databases and URLs. Three things matter when you plan accounts:

- **Non-production stages share a network.** In one AWS account and region, they run in one shared VPC. Each stage has
  its own security groups, so one stage can't reach another stage's databases by private IP. Production stages get a
  VPC of their own. The first deploy decides this, and a stage that becomes production later stays where it is. See
  [Shared network for non-production stages](/configuration/connecting-resources#shared-network-for-non-production-stages).
- **AWS access covers the whole account.** A developer who gets AWS access to one project's account can reach every
  other project's stacks in that account. The Console limits what people see in the Console, not what their AWS
  credentials can do.
- **Separate accounts are the boundary.** Keep production in its own AWS account. An agency keeps one account per
  client and gives each person only their clients' accounts. Service control policies in AWS Organizations then limit
  everything in an account, Stacktape's role included.

## CI/CD

- **Your own pipeline.** GitHub Actions, GitLab CI, Bitbucket, CircleCI or any other CI runs `stacktape deploy` with
  AWS credentials, for example an OIDC role. No Stacktape account is needed. Approvals, environments and release
  gates stay in your CI. `stacktape init` can write the pipeline file. See [Custom CI/CD](/ci-cd-and-gitops/custom-ci-cd).
- **Git deployments in the Console.** A push deploys a stage, and each pull request can get a preview stage that is
  deleted when it closes. Builds run on a runner in your AWS account, one per project, account and region. See
  [Git deployments](/ci-cd-and-gitops/gitops-with-console).
- **Before a deploy.** `stacktape diff` shows what would change. A failed deploy keeps what it already created unless
  you turn on automatic rollback; see [Rollbacks](/deployment-and-lifecycle/rollbacks).

## Scaling, networking and databases

- **Compute.** Containers run on ECS Fargate with minimum and maximum instance counts and CPU or memory targets.
  Functions run on Lambda. Batch jobs and workers cover background work. See [Web service](/resources/compute/web-service#scaling).
- **Databases.** Relational databases run on RDS or Aurora, with Multi-AZ, read replicas, backups kept up to 35 days
  and deletion protection. DynamoDB, Redis, OpenSearch and MongoDB Atlas are also available. See
  [Relational database](/resources/databases/relational-database).
- **Private networking.** A database accepts connections from the internet, with credentials, unless you choose
  otherwise. `accessibilityMode` limits who can connect, and `forceDisablePublicIp` removes the public IP address. On
  Aurora, decide the public IP when you create the database. See
  [Accessibility](/resources/databases/relational-database#accessibility).
- **Regions.** Each stage deploys to one region. Several regions are several stages, and Stacktape doesn't replicate
  data between them. See [Multi-region deployments](/deployment-and-lifecycle/multi-region-deployments).

## What it costs

- **The CLI** is free.
- **The Console** is free while the AWS spend Stacktape manages for your organization stays at or under $200 a month.
  Above that, the Flexible plan costs one rate on the month's managed spend, from 25% down to 19%. Enterprise runs a
  single-tenant Console in your own AWS account, at an agreed price. See
  [Billing and subscription](/stacktape-console/billing-and-subscription).
- **AWS** bills your account for the resources, as it would without Stacktape. `stacktape init` estimates the monthly
  price from AWS list prices before the first deploy, and requests and data transfer come on top. Databases,
  containers, NAT and load balancers cost money every hour they exist, including in preview stages. See
  [Managing costs](/managing-costs/overview).

## Maturity and support

- **Source.** The CLI's code and history are public on [GitHub](https://github.com/stacktape/stacktape), under the MIT
  license. Version 4 is the current major version; [Upgrading from v3](/getting-started/upgrading-from-v3) describes
  the changes.
- **Status.** [status.stacktape.com](https://status.stacktape.com) shows current incidents and maintenance, and their
  history.
- **Support.** Every plan can write to [support@stacktape.com](mailto:support@stacktape.com). The Flexible plan adds a
  shared Slack channel with the founders. Enterprise adds up to 24x7 premium support, with response times agreed in
  advance. No plan comes with a published SLA.
- **AWS problems.** An outage of an AWS service in your account goes through your own AWS Support plan.

## Lock-in and the way out

Everything Stacktape deploys is a regular CloudFormation stack in your account, and `stacktape synth` writes its
template to a file. You can leave the Console and keep the CLI with one command, or take over the stacks and stop
using Stacktape altogether. [Stop using Stacktape](/getting-started/evaluate/stop-using-stacktape) walks through both,
including the helper resources and secrets you would keep or replace.

To bring an app that runs on Heroku, Render, Fly.io, SST or the Serverless Framework, see
[Move to Stacktape](/getting-started/evaluate/move-to-stacktape).

## Run a pilot

1. Create an AWS account for the pilot, or use a development account.
2. Run `stacktape init` in one real service and deploy it. Compare the estimate with your current bill for it.
3. Connect the Console (free) to see deploy history, costs and error tracking for that stage.
4. Deploy a change from your CI, roll it back and restore the database from a backup.
5. Run `stacktape synth` and read the template you would own if you left.
